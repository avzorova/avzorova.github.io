Hidden Economy of Learning

The hidden costs of adult learning

Adult learning often costs more than you are prepared to pay for it. Yes, the budget still exists — but going beyond it can remain unnoticed by the organization.

The cost of correcting errors in the organization of learning rises as work moves from design to practice (the 1–10–100 rule). And it does not matter where learning takes place — at a university or a corporate university, in the workplace or in online courses. The organization’s task is to prevent unjustified cost growth. But that is possible only if you know where exactly you are quietly losing money.

HEL’s approach

The idea of the hidden costs of learning emerged from an analysis of educational practice in Russian enterprises and online schools. Many of them face problems in training staff or clients and look for a separate solution to each. Managers, instructional designers, and instructors identify the same symptoms of ineffective adult learning: lack of transfer, template programs, knowledge transferred apart from the context of its use. Companies face “slower work, more errors, and longer onboarding for newcomers”. In the end a reasonable question arises: “Does learning actually solve anything?”

It does. But effective adult learning is based not on people or metrics, but on scientific knowledge and a systemic approach.

HEL is being developed to improve the effectiveness of adult learning. At the core of the approach is a new view of designing and assessing how learning is organized. The project’s task is to identify and describe gaps between learners’ needs and organizations’ educational conditions. To that end, two aspects of learning are studied:

  • how an adult’s transition from not-knowing to independent action is structured;
  • what has been done so that learning becomes part of their life and professional path.

The result of research within HEL is concrete recommendations for improving the quality of education and learning management. Implementing them helps reduce costs of onboarding, support and individualized learner guidance, repeated training and workplace re-training, and the revision of educational products.

Methodological foundation

HEL develops on the basis of an interdisciplinary approach. The project’s economic foundation is Transaction Cost Economics (Williamson, 1985). Its psychological foundation is research on autonomy and its frustration, psychological reactance, learning engagement, and agency. In particular, HEL draws on Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985), Psychological Reactance Theory (Brehm, 1966), and research on Epistemic Cognition (Greene et al., 2016).

What principally distinguishes HEL from other approaches is its focus on the factors of hidden costs that arise in the process of acquiring and using knowledge in learning and professional activity.

HEL’s central question: at what cost was the educational result obtained — and which part of that cost did not have to be paid?

Current research directions

  • psycho-pedagogical conditions of continuing professional education
  • agency, autonomy, and epistemological beliefs
  • engagement and resistance to learning
  • expert and tacit knowledge, the expert’s blind spot
  • manageable difficulty, epistemic difficulty

Research stages

2023–2026 — dissertation stage completed on a sample of 964 people; educational case analysis carried out in online schools and universities in St. Petersburg.
2026–2027 — development of a questionnaire on forms of response to autonomy restriction; refinement of a scale of learners’ epistemological beliefs; study of learner engagement in organizations.
2027–2028 — theoretical HEL model, research design.
2028–2029 — conducting the experiment.

Manifesto

It is not technology that determines the effectiveness of adult learning. What matters most is a person’s personal and professional experience, needs, interests, and goals. There must be room in the educational process for doubt and disagreement — because without autonomy there is no independent action. A good educational system does not test a person to the limit. Its task is to create conditions in which a person can act as a subject of their own life and professional path.
